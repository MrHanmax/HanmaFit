    Questions

    Calorie Calculator Questions

    How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

    Start from your maintenance, the calories that hold your weight, and eat up to a fifth less. For most people that loses about half a percent of bodyweight a week, mostly fat. Eating far less makes you lose muscle faster and is harder to keep up. The calculator works out your maintenance from your own steps and training and sets the deficit for you.

    How is this different from other calorie calculators?

    Most calculators multiply one equation by an activity level you pick from a list, so two people with very different days get the same number. This one averages eight resting equations, builds your daily burn from your real steps, your work and each kind of training you do, and lets you enter weekdays and weekends separately, because nobody lives the same day seven times a week. It also changes the plan as your weight changes, and two weeks of your own weight and food logs can replace the estimate with your measured maintenance.

    Is it accurate for people in Pakistan and South Asia?

    Yes, as far as any calculator can be. Resting metabolism in South Asian adults runs lower than the European data most equations come from, and the calculator adjusts for that when you choose your ancestry. Any equation can still be about 200 calories out for one person, which is why the plan starts with two weeks that measure your real maintenance.

    How many calories are in roti, rice, daal and biryani?

    Roughly: one medium roti 110 to 130, one paratha 250 to 350, one cup of cooked rice about 200, one bowl of daal 180 to 250, and one plate of biryani 500 to 700 or more. Oil and portion size change these a lot, so weigh portions for a week or two when you start.

    Do I have to hit my calories exactly every day?

    No. Your body works on the weekly average. A wedding, a dawat or a heavy training day is fine when the rest of the week balances it. The plan gives a daily figure and a weekly total, and an optional allowance of discretionary calories for anything you like.

    Can I use it for muscle gain, recomposition, PCOS or diabetes?

    Yes. Choose lose fat, recomposition, build muscle, gain weight or maintain. The health questions change the plan and its warnings for diabetes, thyroid conditions, PCOS, pregnancy and a past eating disorder. It is a nutrition plan, not a diagnosis, so check with your doctor for any medical condition.

    Scope

    Equations estimate; they do not measure. Resting rate varies by about 200 kcal either side of any prediction, which is why every figure carries a range and why two weeks of your own logs beat any further calculation. This is a nutrition plan, not a medical prescription.
